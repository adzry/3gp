# Script — Einstein in your pocket

Narration first; the edit is timed to these words (`npm run voice`, then
`npm run align`). Every figure is in `brief.md` → Sources.

## 1. Cold open

VO: Right now, the phone in your pocket is correcting for Einstein.

## 2. The question

VO: To see why, start with a simpler question. How does it know where you are at all?

## 3. Constellation

VO: About twenty thousand kilometers up, around thirty GPS satellites circle the Earth.

## 4. Time signal

VO: Each one carries an atomic clock, and does one simple thing. It broadcasts the time.

VO: Your phone hears that time a split second late. The signal travels at the speed of light, so the delay is a distance.

## 5. Trilateration

VO: One satellite puts you somewhere on a sphere. A second narrows it to a circle. A third leaves just two points, and only one of them is on the ground. A fourth checks your phone's own clock.

## 6. The microsecond

VO: That only works if the timing is exact. An error of one microsecond is three hundred meters.

## 7. Relativity

VO: And up in orbit, time itself runs differently.

VO: The satellites move fast, which slows their clocks by seven microseconds a day. But they sit higher in Earth's gravity, which speeds them up by forty five.

VO: Net result: thirty eight microseconds gained, every single day.

## 8. Drift

VO: Left uncorrected, your position would drift by about ten kilometers a day.

## 9. The tuned clock

VO: So each clock is tuned to run slightly slow on the ground, so that in orbit, it ticks in step with clocks on Earth.

## 10. Close

VO: Every blue dot on every map is a small, daily proof that Einstein was right.
